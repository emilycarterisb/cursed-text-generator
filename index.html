How to make cursed text

  1. Write your line. Short phrases look best. A name, a threat, a weather report from somewhere that does not exist.
  2. Set the intensity. Low settings add a faint flicker. High settings stack so many marks that letters vanish into the noise.
  3. Choose where the marks go. Marks above give a rising, smoky look. Marks below make the text drip. Marks through the middle give it a scratched, struck-out feel.
  4. Copy and paste. The result is plain Unicode text, so it works in any app that supports Unicode.

What a cursed text generator actually does

A cursed text generator does not draw anything. It takes each letter you type and appends random characters from the Unicode combining block (U+0300 to U+036F) and a few related ranges. Those characters have no width of their own. They attach themselves to the letter before them, so the font stacks them one on top of another and the line seems to bleed out of its box.

Because the effect lives in the text itself, it survives copy and paste. That is the main difference between cursed text and a decorative font, which disappears the moment the text leaves the page that styled it.

Marks above

Accents, hooks and tiny floating letters that pile upward. High levels create the classic towering zalgo look.

Marks through

Overlays and strokes that cross the letter body. They make text look scratched, glitched or censored.

Marks below

Dots, bars and hooks that hang under the baseline. They produce the dripping, melting effect.

Tips for readable curses

Keep the intensity at three or four for anything people need to read, and save the maximum setting for a single word. Put the plain version of important details nearby, because search boxes, screen readers and some username fields cannot handle heavy stacks.

When a platform rejects your text

If an app trims the result, drop the intensity and untick one direction. Light curses that use only marks above or below survive far more often than full ones.

Questions about cursed text

What is cursed text?

Cursed text is ordinary writing that has been corrupted by stacking Unicode combining marks above, through and below each letter. It is also called zalgo text or glitch text, and it looks like it is melting, bleeding or being possessed.

Is cursed text just a font?

No. A font changes how letters are drawn, but the characters underneath stay the same. Cursed text adds extra invisible-width characters to the string itself, so it keeps its look when you paste it into another app.

Where can I paste cursed text?

Anywhere that accepts Unicode: chat apps, social bios, captions, comments, documents and notes. Some apps trim long stacks of marks or reject them in usernames, so test a short sample first.

Why does my cursed text look different in some apps?

Every app and font renders combining marks in its own way. Some clamp the height, some let marks overlap neighbouring lines, and a few strip them completely. Lowering the intensity usually fixes it.

Can I turn cursed text back into normal text?

Yes. Paste the cursed text into the text box and press Clean text. It removes every combining mark and leaves the original letters, numbers and punctuation.

Is cursed text safe for accessibility?

Use it sparingly. Screen readers can stumble over heavy stacks of marks, so keep important information such as contact details, links and instructions in plain text.
